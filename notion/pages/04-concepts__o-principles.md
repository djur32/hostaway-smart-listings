## The short version
- They start from the four pains in the research's problem statement (): repeated edits, content hosts can't read in context, not knowing what goes out, and not knowing whether it arrived. → <mention-page url="https://app.notion.com/p/3eee18af1645814c9da9f6fce430fb5c">Problem statement</mention-page>
- Each one settles a real tension, so it can lose an argument, and each says what it rules out.
- The research rewrote three of the first drafts and added one, facts once.
- Three are floors every direction had to meet (1, 2 and 7). The others are where the directions differed.
- The build simplified two: versions were dropped, so receipts, plus Undo on an accepted tip, are the safety net, and the autonomy dial became Autopilot on or off.
## Seven principles, and where to see them
<table header-row="true">
	<tr>
		<td>Principle</td>
		<td>Rules out</td>
		<td>Where you see it in the prototype</td>
	</tr>
	<tr>
		<td>**1 · Nothing written unseen**</td>
		<td>Silent overwrites; "Saved" that doesn't say where</td>
		<td>Edits wait for Save; a tip changes nothing until Accept; every saved change goes out with a receipt, and an accepted tip can be undone. Autopilot is the host's explicit exception.</td>
	</tr>
	<tr>
		<td>**2 · Facts once** (written as "the home, once")</td>
		<td>A second copy of the facts that drifts</td>
		<td>One value per listing detail in the Listing base, which the Knowledge Hub reads without copying and adds to with the situations behind it. A channel page shows "Uses the Base" until the host customises it.</td>
	</tr>
	<tr>
		<td>**3 · Money and rules vary per channel; voice is a choice**</td>
		<td>One editor for every channel; a Booking.com description box that goes nowhere</td>
		<td>Custom fees, stay rules and policies per channel; tips on Pricing, Availability and Booking policies, not only text; Booking.com's description is set by Booking.com.</td>
	</tr>
	<tr>
		<td>**4 · The AI says only what the home can back**</td>
		<td>Claims the record can't support; explanation used as the only safety</td>
		<td>Every tip's why names its sources. The assistant answers guests only from facts it can cite. Knowledge Hub › Questions marks each guest question answered or not, and when nothing backs an answer the AI asks the host and the gap becomes a tip.</td>
	</tr>
	<tr>
		<td>**5 · Autonomy follows the stakes**</td>
		<td>Autopilot as a default; AI changing money on its own</td>
		<td>Autopilot is off until the host turns it on in a strategy. Even then only text and photo-order tips apply on their own; money and stay rules always wait for Accept.</td>
	</tr>
	<tr>
		<td>**6 · One product for four hosts** (written as "defaults for one, power for many")</td>
		<td>A separate product per host type; a full studio on a new host's first day</td>
		<td>Smart Listings is opt-in per property; one strategy for all channels or one each; four demo accounts, one per host type, use the same screens.</td>
	</tr>
	<tr>
		<td>**7 · Say where, and whether it arrived**</td>
		<td>Sync explained in a paragraph; errors found after publishing</td>
		<td>Every field's caption says where its value comes from ("Uses the Base", "Custom for Airbnb", "ⓘ 3 channels"); receipts show Sent, then Live, or In review on Booking.com.</td>
	</tr>
</table>

## What the research changed
<table header-row="true">
	<tr>
		<td>First draft</td>
		<td>After the research</td>
		<td>Why</td>
	</tr>
	<tr>
		<td>Nothing is lost, everything is findable</td>
		<td>Nothing written unseen</td>
		<td>A restore point answers the wrong moment: hosts want to see a change before it is written</td>
	</tr>
	<tr>
		<td>(none)</td>
		<td>Facts once</td>
		<td>The Knowledge Hub repeats the listing in a copy that drifts, and the assistant answers guests from that copy, not from the listing</td>
	</tr>
	<tr>
		<td>Facts are shared; voice is local</td>
		<td>Money and rules vary per channel; voice is a choice</td>
		<td>Hosts vary fees and minimum stays per channel, almost never the words</td>
	</tr>
	<tr>
		<td>Show the why before the what</td>
		<td>The AI says only what the home can back</td>
		<td>A reason raises acceptance, not scrutiny, so grounding has to be a hard rule</td>
	</tr>
</table>
The evidence for the first, third and fourth leans most on the simulated interviews (). Real host conversations would upgrade them.
## How it was made
Drafted before the research, then reconciled with the research's insights and its diagnosis. Each principle got a tension, a resolution, what it looks like in the UI, and what it rules out.
