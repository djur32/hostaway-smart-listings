	**In one line:** Today the Knowledge Hub repeats the listing in a copy that drifts, "different per channel" is done seven ways, and each channel plays by its own rules. So Smart Listings starts from one shared record, with a page group per channel on top and tips that respect each channel.

## What came out of it
- **The Knowledge Hub repeats the listing, and has no job of its own.** It keeps its own copy, generated from Details and past messages, and later edits don't reach it. The assistant answers guests from that copy, and nobody sees what guests ask. → Listing details live once, in the Listing base in **Shared**. The Hub reads them, adds the situations behind them, and learns from guests' questions in the inbox.
- **No view of one listing on one channel.** Per-channel values are spread over about 15 fields in seven different patterns. → Each channel gets its own pages, and every field says whether it uses the Base, is customised for that channel, exists only on that channel, or is set by the channel.
- **Channels don't just differ in limits. They differ in who writes the listing.** Booking.com writes its own description. → Its description is read-only in Smart Listings, and tips are written per channel.
- **The only listing AI today is Airbnb's.** Hostaway's AI answers guests; listing recommendations come from Airbnb's Opportunities. → Smart Listings brings tips to every channel, grounded in the Listing base and in what guests ask.
- **The unknowns are written down.** The riskiest assumptions (trust in AI tips, who wants per-channel copy, what Autopilot may touch) each have a planned test. None has been tested with hosts.
