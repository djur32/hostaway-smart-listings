## The short version
- **Managing a listing is scattered.** The real version lives on Airbnb, in a document or a sheet, and the AI answers from a store no channel sees.
- **It's unclear where an update lands.** Saving says nothing about where it went, so hosts want to see a change before it's written, and proof after.
- **Each channel differs, so one strategy won't fit.** Money and stay rules differ per channel, not words. Copy per channel is one persona's job.
## 1 · Managing a listing is scattered (Strong)
- Each simulated host names a different real version: Airbnb (Marta, Priya), a private document (Dana), a team sheet (Callum). Rob drew the arrows both ways.
- The Knowledge Hub keeps its own copy of the listing, and "Ask the AI" answers from that copy. A simulated host files "what the AI knows" with guest information, not with the home, so H-17 is refuted as written.
- On the trial listing, the Hub told a guest "Yes, the apartment is wheelchair accessible", with no amenity saying so (`REAL·PRODUCT`).
**In the design:** listing details live once, in the Listing base. The Knowledge Hub reads them and adds the situations behind them. <mention-page url="https://app.notion.com/p/3eee18af1645813ab697f55d6d253754">KD-02</mention-page>
## 2 · It's unclear where an update lands (Strong)
- Dana: "I press save and... it saved. Saved where? Did Airbnb take it?"
- Callum: "Show me what you're going to change before you change it. A dry run."
- Marta: "When I imagine it on my real flat, I get nervous."
- On the product, Save gives no per-channel result, no history and no undo (`REAL·PRODUCT`).
**In the design:** a canvas shows each channel's guest page with the change on it, before save. <mention-page url="https://app.notion.com/p/3eee18af1645819e9675db2668610c2b">KD-14</mention-page> Each save sends a receipt per channel. <mention-page url="https://app.notion.com/p/3eee18af164581a693f6c6cc544b8c1a">KD-15</mention-page> This is the finding that changed the design most.

## 3 · Each channel differs (Strong for money and rules, Moderate for copy)
- The cleaning fee is overridden per channel on 36% of multi-channel properties, the minimum stay on 30%, and no text field goes above 4.1%. The overrides are simulated; the fields are the product's own (`REAL·PRODUCT`).
- Only the SEO optimiser writes different copy for each channel. Callum wants one good text, adapted to each channel's rules. The sample is small, so this is Moderate.
- On Booking.com the description is written by the channel, from the host's facts (`REAL·PUBLIC`). Two simulated hosts accept that when they see a preview first.
**In the design:** money and stay rules per channel in one pattern. <mention-page url="https://app.notion.com/p/3eee18af16458172b17ac84a5abb32ef">KD-11</mention-page> Voice per channel is opt-in. <mention-page url="https://app.notion.com/p/3eee18af1645819d96d4cff8530e1600">KD-05</mention-page> One strategy per channel, made in the wizard. <mention-page url="https://app.notion.com/p/3eee18af164581028d64c3d0ff9f7d48">KD-06</mention-page>

## What this does not show
- Five simulated interviews and simulated data. These findings say what to test first, not how common each one is. A real round of about 25 interviews has to test them.
- The Knowledge Hub finding is refuted as written, so it needs a test before anything is built on it.
## How it was made
Simulated interviews, written to a spec, each synthesised on one template, then combined. Product and public evidence was checked against the live prototype and the help centre. Import findings are left out, because the case covers Smart Listings only.
