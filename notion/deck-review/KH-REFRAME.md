# The Knowledge Hub, reframed (Antonio, 4 Oct)

Binding for the deck, the prototype and Notion.

## What Antonio said, condensed
- The "two truths" reasoning points the right way, but it is framed incorrectly. "14 of 28 facts match" is specific to his trial account: importing a listing copies its amenities. The story must be **universal**: how the functionality works, for any host.
- Today the Knowledge Hub has **no clear purpose and no product direction**:
  - It restates what the listing already holds (amenities, rules, check-in), so it repeats the listing.
  - "Ask the AI" isn't faithful to reality.
  - It's unclear what it does for the listing.
- The Hub must have **its own job**: answering questions, and giving insight into what guests are asking or talking about, so Hospitable learns more.
  - It **reads the facts from the Listing base**, the source of truth, instead of holding its own copy.
  - Questions come from **interactions**, e.g. the Inbox.
  - What it learns feeds **tips**: additions, changes and improvements to the listing.
  - So tips also live in the Hub, part of bringing tips to other product areas.
- The "Two truths" detail ("why the two drift apart") confused him. Rethink it as a universal explanation.

## Today, universal (every claim needs its source)
1. **The Hub keeps its own copy of the listing.**
   - It is generated on request from Details and past messages.
   - Later edits to Details don't reach it, so the copy drifts by design.
   - Source: Hospitable help centre, Knowledge Hub page, REAL·PUBLIC; the URL is in `plan/notion-v2/pages/01-o-today.md`.
2. **It repeats the listing.** It restates amenities, rules and check-in as its own sentences, beside the fields that already hold them. REAL·PRODUCT, observed on a trial listing: say "on a trial listing", never as a universal count.
3. **"Ask the AI" answers from that copy, not from the listing**, so it can state what nothing backs. Example, labelled as observed on a trial listing: "Yes, the apartment is wheelchair accessible" with no amenity saying so. REAL·PRODUCT.
4. **No one sees what guests ask.** Guest questions live in the Inbox, and nothing turns them into knowledge or into improvements to the listing. REAL·PRODUCT, from the product audit.

## The Hub's new job
1. **Facts are read, not copied.** Amenities, rules, check-in, Wi-Fi and so on live once in the Listing base. The Hub shows them by reference ("from the Listing base") and never keeps a second version.
2. **Questions.** Every guest question from the Inbox is collected:
   - grouped by topic (parking, check-in, Wi-Fi…);
   - counted, per channel and over time;
   - marked answered or unanswered.
3. **Answers stay faithful.** The guest AI answers only from facts in the Base and answers the host confirmed. When nothing backs an answer, it says so and asks the host. It never guesses.
4. **Insights.** What guests ask most, what's new this month, and what one channel's guests ask more than another's.
5. **Gaps become tips.** A frequent or unanswered question becomes a tip:
   - add the fact to the Listing base, or say it in a channel's description;
   - the host's answer becomes a fact in the Base, so every channel and the guest AI use it.
   - The tips use the same tip contract as everywhere (field, why, sources, accept or undo, receipt), cited by the questions behind them.
6. **Sources of the Hub:** the Listing base (read); Inbox conversations (questions and answers); the host (describing a fact); documents such as a guidebook (Sources tab).

## Words
- Call it the Knowledge Hub.
- Its sections: **Questions · Insights · Facts (from the Listing base) · Sources**.
- In the deck, never use "two truths" as the frame any more. Use "the Hub repeats the listing and has no job of its own" as today's problem, and "the Hub learns from guests' questions" as the direction.
