This is the test design. Every bar is written before its step runs, so nobody can move the goalposts after seeing a result.

It starts with today’s product. Forty hosts do the same tasks, so better means better than today. Then a tree test of three menus, two think-aloud rounds of eight hosts, a comparison of today against channel-first, a six-week pilot, and a staged rollout with five percent held out.

[Press] Each step has its bar. Seventy percent on the channel tasks. Time to adjust at seventy-five or less. Task success up ten points with time not worse. Eighty percent satisfied. Guardrails holding. Accessibility gates every step.

We considered one big launch test instead. We rejected it, because a failure there cannot say which part failed. The constraint is that the same tasks run at every step, so the results compare. The probes, and what a missed bar decides, are one click away. About 45 seconds.

This is how the concept is measured. One north star: listings with a strategy-aligned field on every active channel. A host who sets a strategy and ends up with fields that match each channel has done what the product is for.

Under it sit four branches. Adoption, measured by hosts who set a strategy and tips accepted. Quality, measured by fields tailored per channel and by violations per 100 applied tips. Outcome, measured by booking conversion per channel and time to finish an edit. And guardrails: undo rate, tickets, active editors, listing views and load time.

[Press] Each branch names the bet it tests and the experiment that moves it. Strategy per channel moves adoption. Tips with a why moves acceptance. Per-channel rewrite moves edit time. Autopilot is held to an undo rate under eight percent. The numbers on the cards are the smallest changes worth shipping, not results.

We considered revenue per listing as the north star and rejected it, because it moves slowly and cannot say which change helped. The constraint is that the host account is the unit, since a host’s listings share tips. About 45 seconds.
