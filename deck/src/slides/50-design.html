Today the Hub has no job of its own, so I gave it one: guests’ questions. It no longer keeps a copy of the listing. It reads the listing details from the Listing base and adds the situations the host writes. It has four sections: Questions, Insights, Facts and Sources. It starts in the Inbox. A guest asks if there’s free parking, and the question lands in the Hub. It’s grouped, counted, and marked answered or not. This one was asked nine times and never answered. The assistant only answers from what backs it. When nothing backs an answer, it says so and asks the host.

[Press] That gap becomes a tip, with the same tip format as everywhere else. Its why is the questions behind it. It goes to one of two places. A shared fact for the Listing base, like free parking. Or a better answer in one channel’s listing, like Airbnb’s description.

[Press] When the host accepts, the answer is saved where it belongs. A listing detail goes in the Listing base, so every channel and the assistant use it. A situation goes in the Hub. Either way, the next guest gets an accurate answer. To be clear, the Hub is a concept, not built in full. It shows how the Hub can feed the tips. The counts are demo data. The full flow is one click away. About 45 seconds.

Pattern: Hostaway’s Add-property wizardSuggestion evidence, demo dataThe three strategies

Smart Listings is opt-in, switched on per property, because not every host wants AI on their listing. With it off, Shared and Channels look exactly the same, so the structure serves every host. Turning it off later keeps everything. Turning it on opens a wizard hosts already know: Hostaway’s Add-property flow. So I reused it rather than invent a new pattern. First, what to aim for. Each option shows its evidence, like this one: 8 of your 13 empty nights on Airbnb are Monday to Thursday.

[Press] Then the guardrails, which are the contract. Smart Listings never goes outside the price range. Prices, stay rules and cancellation always ask. That lock can’t be turned off.

[Press] Last, whether Autopilot may act. It starts off. There’s one strategy per channel, or one shared by several. So Airbnb and Booking.com can go after different guests. About 55 seconds.

DesignCanvas preview

See the listing as guests will, then tap to change it

Hostaway publishes with no preview today. I rebuilt each channel’s real guest page. A tap opens the field behind it, its tip or Rewrite with AI.

  • Purple: a tip waits here
  • Orange: editable here
  • No colour: owned elsewhere
  • Show only editable sections: hide the rest
  • Your edits or Live now: compare, and go back to the original

Classic or Canvas, one switch

Changes publish without a preview

Canvas goes beyond a preview. One switch shows only the sections you can edit. Another switches between your edits and what’s live on the channel now. So you can see the difference, and go back to the original with one click. Today no screen in Hostaway shows one listing as it looks on one channel, and changes publish without a preview. So Canvas shows the channel’s real guest page, and colour says what you can do. Purple: a tip waits here. Orange: you can edit it here. No colour: it’s owned somewhere else. On Airbnb the title has a purple ring, because a tip waits on it.

[Press] Booking.com gets its own page, one switch away from Classic. Canvas works for every host, with Smart Listings on or off. My first version let you edit right on the page. In review, that became a second editor for the same field, so I took it out. Now a tap opens the field itself, and a price opens Pricing’s own fields. One field, one editor. About 50 seconds.

DesignAutopilot

Autopilot for words and photos. Money always asks you

With Autopilot on, text and photo-order tips apply inside the host’s guardrails, since they are cheap to undo. Prices, stay rules and cancellation always wait for a yes.

The editor says Autopilot is on

The Autopilot question

In my sampled interviews, hosts feared losing money and status far more than a sentence. So I made control follow the stakes. Autopilot is off by default, and the host chooses it per strategy. When it’s on, it may rewrite a sentence or reorder photos inside the host’s guardrails, because those are cheap to undo.

[Press] Anything that changes money, who can book, or what guests are promised always asks. That lock can’t be turned off.

[Press] With Autopilot on, the editor says so, here on Pricing, and offers the way out. I first designed a three-level dial per content type. One switch turned out simpler. In the prototype, the switch and its states are built. The automatic applying itself isn’t simulated. About 50 seconds.

Building Smart Listings, I didn’t only add to Hostaway’s design system. I challenged parts of it. Four matter most.

First, drill-down navigation. Only the main menu had it. Now each section opens to its own pages. Second, counts that move.
