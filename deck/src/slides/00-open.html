IntroScope and goals

The scope is an intelligent listing. We took it deeper.

We started from two questions. What does a listing look like when each channel can be different? And how much control do hosts need? Then we went deeper, in four ways. [Press] First, from copy to strategy. Every channel gets its own view and its own goal, not just new words. Second, from the text to the whole listing. The tips cover pricing, availability and booking policies too. They’re built on data: guests’ questions, the facts about the home, and similar homes. Third, and this matters most, you choose how much freedom it has. You can review every tip yourself. Or you can switch on an optional Autopilot that works inside your guardrails. You decide how much you stay in the loop. Fourth, from launch to results. Every strategy is tracked over time, against the weeks before it and week by week as it changes. So hosts keep what works. The alternative was a smarter text box on each channel. It answers the first question but not how much control hosts need, so we went wider. About 40 seconds.

Our hypothesis

If every channel gets its own strategy, and recommendations backed by data, listing management stops being a chore and becomes Hostaway's competitive advantage.

It's true when

Changes take less time Hosts trust what goes out Hosts keep the tips More bookings on every channel

So here is what we want to prove. Give every channel its own strategy, and back the tips with data. Then listing management stops being a chore. It becomes Hostaway’s edge over its competitors. We’ll know it’s true when four things happen. Changes take less time. Hosts trust what goes out. They keep the tips they’re given. And each channel beats its own baseline. We come back to these four when we show how the design measures it. We framed the hypothesis around outcomes, not features. The alternative was “AI writes better copy”, but copy is only one part of the listing. The constraint is that the proof points need Hostaway’s own data, so the design defines them and leaves the numbers open. About 25 seconds.

How we set them

Here is what the design is meant to move, and how it shows. First, more bookings and booking requests per listing, against its own weeks before. Hostaway already has that data. The example target is a modest 5% after 30 days and 7% after 90. We know the season moves these numbers, so the bar stays modest. Second, adoption: eligible properties with a strategy on at least one channel. 10%, then 25%, on the way to a third at six months. Third, hosts keep improving: of the hosts who turned it on, how many act on a tip each month. Fourth, tips earn their place: 30% accepted at first, 40% by 90 days. And under all of it, fewer listing and sync tickets per hundred properties. These are example targets, set from the logic of the design. Hostaway’s own baselines would replace them. IntroProcessWith AI

The process we followHow we agentify it

Six stages, each with the methods we use.Agents read and make at every stage. At every gate, we decide.

Two parts. First, the process we follow. These are the six stages, with the methods under each. Discovery is a product audit and heuristic evaluation, desk research on channels and competitors, assumption mapping and stakeholder interviews. Research is host interviews, data analysis and tree testing. Concept is seven directions on a weighted matrix, plus concept testing. Design is the prototype in code, reviewed round after round, the design system and an accessibility review. Testing is a benchmark test, usability tests and A/B tests, each defined before it runs. Systems is mapping, second-order effects, rollout and pilot design, and kill criteria. We chose stages over one big design sprint because each stage retires a risk before the next spends effort. The constraint is access to hosts and data, so the research design says what each method asks and leaves the answers open. Second, how we run it with AI agents. At each stage, something goes in. One of our skills does the reading or the making. Then there’s a gate where we decide. Which hypotheses matter. Whether it’s a pattern or a loud few. Which direction. Whether the system holds. What a finding means. And what goes first. The agents carry the work. The decisions stay ours. About a minute.
