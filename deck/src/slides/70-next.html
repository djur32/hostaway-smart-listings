Why this story

This slide works on two levels. First, what hosts hear: your listings, handled, and you still have the last word. Your facts live once. Every channel gets tips tuned to its own strategy, from the assistant. The assistant can apply tips within your guardrails, but money always asks, and every change gets a receipt.

[Press] Second, where the product goes. This is not a feature launch. It reorganises the property around one Base, puts the assistant wherever the listing is used, and makes Smart Listings a capability across Hostaway. The next slide shows where it spreads.

I considered telling it as a feature tour and rejected it, because a tour describes screens and not what changes for the host. The constraint is that money always asks, so the story never promises hands-off pricing. About 40 seconds.

Smart Listings only pays off as a system, not a screen. In the middle is Shared, which is the Listing base and the Knowledge Hub that reads it, with Smart Listings on top. The inner ring is the core, drawn in the prototype on sample data. Tips and the Base reach Airbnb, Booking.com and Direct. The guest assistant answers from the same facts. And results come back as Performance.

The next ring extends it, and I name the open edges. Four lines of work are implied but not drawn. One: similar homes for each channel. Two: a deeper Knowledge Hub whose job is guests’ questions. It answers only from facts, shows what guests ask, turns gaps into tips, and knows which messages and tasks use each fact. Three: Performance a host can act on over time. Four: tips beyond the listing, in the Calendar and the Inbox first, all with one tip format. Import joins here, on the same Base.

The outer ring is reach. Pricing reads the strategy’s goals. Owner statements read the receipts. And the Base’s facts and the Hub’s answers feed the Guidebook, Operations, Devices, Google and the API.

I chose rings over a list because the order of dependence matters more than the date. The constraint is that every ring reuses the same fact, source and tip patterns. How each line of work runs is one click away. About 70 seconds.

The design hands off in one week, in four packs: the design spec and prototype, the channel rules and tip contract, the experiment portfolio, and the events, flags and kill switch. Monday is the kick-off with product and the listing team. Every owner gets their open questions in writing. [Press] Tuesday, Integrations confirms what each channel accepts. AI and ML confirm what feeds the Knowledge Hub. Data fixes the metric definitions. [Press] Wednesday, hypotheses, minimum effects and decision rules are written for all seven experiments, and Data computes the sample sizes. [Press] Thursday, CS and Legal review the guardrails, the AI copy and the channel terms. [Press] Friday, a one-page readout to every team with owners, dates and open questions.

I chose a week of short, named handoffs over one long review, because each team can answer its own questions in a day. The constraint is that every experiment needs an owner before it can start. About 45 seconds.
