Where each came from

Before designing any direction, we wrote the rules to judge them by. Seven principles, each settling a real tension, so each one can lose an argument. In one line each: nothing changes until Accept. One value per detail. Fees and minimum stays vary per channel, words rarely. Every tip names its sources. Money always waits for Accept. Opt-in per property. Receipts show Sent, then Live.

[Press] we wrote six first, then let the design work edit them. Three got rewritten, and one was added: facts once. The alternative was a longer list of rules. We kept seven because each one has to be able to lose an argument, and a rule that never loses decides nothing.

[Press] Number three shaped everything. Our premise is that fees and minimum stays differ per channel, and the words rarely do. So per channel is a main pattern for money and rules. A separate voice is a choice, not a chore. That became the backbone of Smart Listings. About 45 seconds.

Round one asked how a host could think about a listing at all. We wanted directions that were truly different. So we set a hard bar: no two could share a mental model, a main object or a first action. Here they are, each drawn as the thing you’d work on. The channel’s own page. A master spreadsheet. A conversation. An inbox of decisions. A version history. A house manual. A floor plan. Every frame used the same flat in Hostaway’s design system, so they could be compared.

[Press] No model won every screen. The Channel Canvas scored best where a host shapes each channel’s page. The Decision Inbox scored best where a host runs many homes. So we kept them as a pair. One answers "what will guests see?" The other answers "what needs us?" The frames and our scores are one click away. The scores are our design judgement against fifteen criteria, so read them as a reasoned ranking and not as measurements. The criteria come from the principles: facts once, control per channel, trust in the AI, effort for the host, and how well it works on a phone. About 45 seconds.

Same flow

Round one gave us a structure. Round two was about the listing itself. How should AI help a host make each channel’s listing better? We built twelve directions in Paper. Each one uses a pattern people already trust, from Google Ads’ optimisation score to Notion’s suggesting mode. We saved the references beside each one. Borrowing what hosts already know means less to learn. Every direction ran the same flow, from the overview through pricing, availability and policies to receipts. And the same hard rules applied. Money and stay rules never go on autopilot. An unknown fact becomes a question, never a guess.

[Press] Two became the core of the build. The live canvas is now the Canvas view. The assistant became the docked tips panel and Rewrite with AI. Four lent a part: who the strategy is for, its goal and guardrails, the tracked change with its why, and similar homes as evidence. About 50 seconds.

      How it converged

      Here’s how two rounds became one model. Round one gave us the shape. The inbox’s "what needs you" became tips. The canvas became a window on each channel’s real page. Round two gave the parts: the live canvas, the assistant, two strategy directions, the tracked change, and similar homes as evidence.

      [Press] Put together, that’s Smart Listings. A strategy per channel: who it’s for, a goal and guardrails. Tips with their why, one at a time, in a docked panel. The Canvas view of each channel’s real page. And Rewrite with AI on any field without a tip. It needs no strategy, so it works even with Smart Listings off.
