I started by mapping today's product, from a real trial account with a real Airbnb listing in Berlin. Everything hangs off the property: ten sections, from Overview to Direct.

[Press] And the listing, the thing a guest reads, isn't one of them. Each connected channel, Airbnb, Booking.com or Direct, is a row on Overview, and none gets a page of its own.

[Press] What's different per channel is scattered instead: about fifteen fields across five sections, handled seven different ways. In my heuristic evaluation that breaks per-channel awareness at severity 4, the top of Nielsen's scale.

[Press] Then the facts live twice: the Knowledge Hub keeps its own copy, in its own words, for the guest AI. That breaks source-of-truth clarity and AI transparency, both at 4. And Calendar leaves the property for the global Calendar, with no way back. The severities are my own judgement, so I treat them as assumptions.

[Press] Today the only listing advice is Airbnb's own Opportunities. So each channel's listing gets its own pages, with tips on every channel. The full map, and how I made it, sit in the detail. About 50 seconds.

This is the slide the whole case rests on. When I looked at one real Airbnb listing in Hospitable, Details and the Knowledge Hub agreed on only 14 of 28 facts. Land the number: 14 of 28. In the heuristic evaluation that breaks source-of-truth clarity, at severity 4.

[Press] Take the house rules: they exist three times, a paragraph in the wrong field, an empty field in the right place, and six sentences only the AI reads.

[Press] Then I asked the AI whether the flat is wheelchair accessible. It said yes, which the listing never claims, and showed no source: AI transparency, also a 4. Same flat, two answers. This isn't a copy problem; the product is built to drift, and the detail shows why.

[Press] So in Smart Listings every fact lives once, in Shared, where the channels and the guest AI read the same one. About 50 seconds.

The second structural gap is the channel view. If a host wants to know what their Airbnb listing says right now, they check about fifteen fields, one info icon at a time, like this one on Fees. In the heuristic evaluation that breaks per-channel awareness, at severity 4.

[Press] Hospitable can hold different values per channel, but only field by field, through seven different patterns, which breaks consistency and standards, a 3. And exactly one screen, Instant book, treats the channel as an axis.

[Press] And here is the trap: opening Edit per channel just to look marks the form as changed, so a host who only looked and then saved has created overrides.

[Press] So each channel gets its own pages in Smart Listings, and every field there is one of four cases: it uses the Base, it's custom for that channel, it exists only on that channel, or the channel sets it itself, like the description Booking.com writes, which is read-only with a lock that says why. Looking never changes anything. About 45 seconds.

The brief gave me one real number: 618 sales calls across 457 accounts, with listing management as a top theme. I built simulated breakdowns on top to show how I'd read a real export, so read these as shapes, not sizes.

[Press] In my sample, listing trouble comes up in 29% of calls with the smallest hosts and climbs to 51% at 25 properties or more.

[Press] It likely costs deals: closed-lost calls raise it 58% of the time, against 36% of closed-won.

[Press] And support: 71% of content-overwritten tickets escalate. The expensive ones are content overwritten and prices not syncing, moments where Hospitable wrote to a channel without showing the host. The product confirms that part for real: editing the base price "will overwrite the price on Airbnb", said once in a paragraph, and Save never says what reached each channel. In the heuristic evaluation that breaks visibility of system status, a severity 3.

[Press] So in Smart Listings every saved change goes out with a receipt. About 55 seconds.

Booking.com and Hospitable help pages

I turned my thesis into hypotheses, each with what would disprove it, and scored them by importance and evidence. These are the thirteen that decide Smart Listings. Write-back to Booking.com ranked second, until Hospitable's help centre and Booking.com's partner docs showed that Booking.com writes the description itself.

[Press] So it left the list and became a design constraint: on Booking.com, a host's voice is the quality of their facts.

[Press] What I test first: whether a why, a source and an undo earn trust in AI tips, who wants a different voice on each channel, and what hosts will let Autopilot touch. Almost nobody asks for AI listing copy in public, so that demand is a bet too. Three of my first riskiest five were about import; they wait for the next release. About 50 seconds.

Week one is data first, because exports need no recruiting: call and ticket analysis, how hosts use Edit per channel and the Knowledge Hub today, and ten stakeholder interviews with Hospitable's own teams.
