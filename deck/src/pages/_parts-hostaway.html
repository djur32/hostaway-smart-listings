Source of truth

“2 channels”: the reach, never a surprise

Product audit

The structural problem is that Hostaway already holds two truths, and they agree on half the facts. So I made the Base one clean layer, labelled as the source of truth: the values every channel inherits, and nothing that belongs to one channel. Look closer at a field: each one tells you how many channels it reaches, two for the name, one for the summary, so an edit is never a surprise. This is also what makes everything after it possible: tips, strategies and receipts all start from one trusted record. Where I challenge today's product: channel-only fields sit among shared ones, and Edit per channel turns every tab into a per-channel editor.

Classic or Canvas, one switch

Hostaway today: publish without a previewConcept test with eight hosts

My original idea was a preview you can click, and the test told me where it shines and where it is dangerous. Canvas shows the listing as guests will see it, and colour tells you what you can do: lavender, a tip waits here; raspberry, editable here; no colour, owned elsewhere. It is the channel's real page, Airbnb or Booking.com, one switch away from Classic, because copywriters want the page beside the text. But the friendliest surface is also where new hosts stop reading, so nothing that changes money is decided here. Canvas is a way to find a field, and every field still has exactly one editor.

The editor says Autopilot is on

The Autopilot question

Control is not a slider of trust; I made it follow the stakes. Autopilot may rewrite a sentence or reorder photos inside the price range the host set, because those are cheap to undo. Anything that changes money, who can book or what guests are promised always asks, and that lock cannot be turned off. With Autopilot on, the editor says so, here on Pricing, and offers the way out, so the host is never surprised by their own listing.
